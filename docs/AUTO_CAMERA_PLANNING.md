# Automatic camera planning

Scene analysis resolves each wire using `worldPin`, `worldPinDirection` and `makeWireCurve`. Forty equal-arc-length samples include the saved manual bends and exit stubs. The export scene supplies actual world bounding boxes after CAD master center, origin, orientation, scaling and component transform. A rotated dimension/origin fallback is available to pure planning tests. Hidden parts are excluded.

Each subject has a previous-shot candidate plus 64 golden-angle directions spanning azimuth, elevation and some underside views. Camera positions and targets derive from current geometry, not the starter circuit. A 38-degree perspective camera is fitted to every relevant point in its own basis at 16:9, with horizontal/vertical safe margins. Overview uses whole-scene bounds and candidates from the front, elevated hemisphere, matching the editor workbench orientation. The opening and closing share this upright camera. Connection shots use the full route; complementary shots focus on physical endpoints.

The deterministic coarse score combines source/destination visibility (dominant), endpoint screen separation, projected subject area, component-center recognizability, frame margin, elevation, logarithmic distance penalty and previous view continuity. Occlusion uses ray intersections with solid component bounding boxes and an endpoint tolerance of 1.4 mm. Only the top eight candidates receive sampled route-visibility refinement. Camera diagnostics are retained on each shot and appear in the preview shot selector. The same snapshot and settings always produce the same scores.

Continuity gets a three-point weight and the previous direction is itself a candidate, giving a preference for retaining a useful angle. Camera movement is completed before a stable reveal. If both endpoints cannot be considered clear, the planner warns, adds a source view, frames the full route for reveal, and adds destination inspection. It does not label the obstructed pair as fully visible. Warnings remain accessible after export.

Interpolation uses a smoothstep target and normalized direction/radius interpolation, a fixed world-up direction, and an exterior scene-sphere constraint during movement. No barrel roll is introduced. This is a conservative safety approximation, not a full collision-free path solver for arbitrary concave/enclosing scenes. Extreme opposite-angle overrides and very large or enclosing objects may require a manual shot override. The scene is static during offline rendering.

Planning is performed once per generated sequence, never per output frame. Bounds avoid full CAD triangle raycasts. Route samples are reused across candidate scoring and overlay evaluation. Complexity scales with candidate count × eligible wires × visible parts; no persistent cross-project plan cache is maintained, so changed geometry cannot reuse a stale plan.

Limitations: bounding boxes can report false obstruction around hollow connectors and may miss small detailed obstructions. No photorealistic pixel visibility guarantee or text-silkscreen recognition is claimed. Screen wire separation is approximated by endpoint separation and sampled-route visibility, rather than a full pairwise wire-overlap raster. Labels use reserved lanes to avoid mutual overlap; leader lines may cross geometry. Inspect preview for dense or unusual circuits.
